# Sizer 3.0.0 plan (APPROVED by Ben 2026-10-15)

A copy of the approved plan (`/Users/benschofield/.claude/plans/cozy-orbiting-swing.md` on the Mac), kept here so every machine sees it. Phase-gated: stop and report after each phase. Progress is in `docs/STATUS.md`.


## Context

The bead calibration of 2026-10-15 (analysis on the LaCie: `Calibration/Imaging Work/analysis/2026-10-15/`;
deck "Bead calibration: what we tried, what failed") showed what the current sizer (2.2.0) gets wrong:

- **The focus test is far too loose.**
  - "In focus" is core T ≤ 0.70 plus the 2.2.0 sharpness rule. It counts beads as in focus over 9 / 12 / 15 mm of
    depth (37 / 56 / 76 µm).
  - Size holds within 5% over only 0.8 / 1.2 / 2.5 mm of that.
  - Falling beads were counted in focus out to a blur width of χ ≈ 60 µm, where they read 30–50% large.
- **The blur width χ is the right test.**
  - χ is ~13 µm at best focus, growing ~8 µm per mm, much the same for every size.
  - Falling beads lie on the static through-focus curve: they are simply beads seen off focus.
  - A cut at χ ≤ 15–20 µm brings their sizes back to the at-focus values.
- **At focus the half-max edge reads a few µm large.**
  - The sharpest falling beads (56–155.8 µm) read a fixed **+6 µm** (fit slope 1.002), so the edge, not the scale,
    is the likelier cause.
  - 10 µm/px is recorded as calibrated (STATUS "Settled"), and the beads agree.
- **The AI calls far-out-of-focus spheres "filament" or "blob"**, so pure spheres read 62–89% atomised by area.
- **Over half the spray is below our smallest bead.** On CCD run 125754, 52% of the in-focus droplets read under
  37 µm. The bottom 5% sit at the 4 px floor (22.6 µm).
- **Focus was set by eye, and it misses.** The 155.8 µm slide was ~1 mm off focus (χ 21 µm, against 13). Nothing
  records where the focal plane sits relative to the spray.

**Constraints and decisions so far**
- Ben (2026-10-15): with a calibrated focus rule, the size special cases go (the sharpness rule from 50 µm up, and
  the AI mask below 40 µm).
- No smaller beads until a better 3× lens. The PS20 reticle is coming.
- Keep 2.2.0 results alongside the new ones.
- Re-measure from the saved predictions; never re-run the AI. Every run kept its frames and predictions.

**Decided with Ben (2026-10-15):**
- **One χ_max for every size** (Blaisot): the measurement depth is then the same at every size.
- **Atomised fraction:** counts only what is inside the focus slab, with today's all-objects figure kept alongside.
- **Droplets below d_min:** counted (concentration, atomised fraction) but not sized.
- **"Confident" for d_min:**
  - the median size bias within ±10% after the edge correction;
  - ≥ 90% of droplets of that size found and passed by the focus rule;
  - scatter (RMS) reported per size, but not used to set d_min.
- **The defocus look-up table comes now, in 3.0.0** (Phase 3), and is kept as tier 2 if it passes the falling-bead
  check. When the reticle arrives it is improved: certified opaque dots added, extended below 37 µm. That is profile
  v2, with no code change.

**How each phase reads:**
- **Why** it is needed.
- **Where it comes from:** the evidence.
- **When the reticle arrives:** what it adds.

The work is phase-gated: stop and report after each phase. Phases 1 and 2 can run alongside each other.

## Phase 1. Focus truly on the spray centre (Ben, 2026-10-15)

**What, rig** (Ben, 2026-10-16: a **300 µm precision pinhole**, arriving the week of 2026-10-19; the camera focuses on
a rail; the field starts more than 50 mm below the exit; a holder can seat in the insert holder).
- **The focus target: a Ø300 µm precision pinhole,** a hole in thin metal foil in a ~1" mount.
  - **The holder** seats in the insert holder in place of an orifice insert and carries the pinhole down into the
    imaging field, with the foil plane on the orifice axis, facing the camera.
  - **Why a foil pinhole suits this:** the foil is only microns thick, with no glass, so the feature plane is the foil
    itself. There is no "which face" question and no refraction offset, which a dot on glass would have.
  - **It looks reversed:** a bright disc on a black frame, because the mount blocks the backlight across the field.
    The meter reads the edge of the bright disc.
  - **Why 300 µm:** about 30 px across with ~94 px of edge, so the blur width averages over many pixels and reads
    steadily. At 23× the in-focus blur, the edge behaves like a straight edge. Depth sensitivity (~8 µm of blur per
    mm) does not depend on size.
- **The rotation check still works.** The foil is thin and symmetric, so either face can face the camera.
  - Find best focus, rotate the holder 180° about the vertical axis, and find best focus again.
  - A foil off the axis by e moves to −e, so the axis plane is midway between the two readings.
  - If the two differ by more than ~0.2 mm, re-seat or adjust the holder.
- **Squareness hardly matters for a small target.** A 2° tilt puts the two sides of a 300 µm disc only ~10 µm apart
  in depth, well inside the ±0.25 mm aimed for.
- **A scale on the camera rail** (a digital scale or micrometer head) so every focus move is read to ~0.01 mm and can
  be repeated.
- **Take the holder out before spraying:** silicone mist would clog the pinhole.

**What, code.**
- **`AI/Real_Data_Code/focus_edge.py`:** the blur width of an edge.
  - **For a disc** (the pinhole: bright on dark; or a dot: dark on bright): find the centre, then take the radial
    edge profile averaged all the way round the circle. That is very precise.
  - **For a straight edge** (a reticle square, a razor blade): the profile across the edge, averaged along it.
  - Fit the edge-spread function (an erf). For our Gaussian PSF, χ = (10–90% edge width) / 1.28.
  - It also measures the disc's diameter against its certificate: a scale check to ~1% at 30 px.
- **A "Focus check" panel in GUI_Clean:**
  - shows the pinhole's χ live and the best value so far;
  - shows green when χ ≤ χ0 + 2 µm (within ~±0.25 mm of focus);
  - converts χ to a "|z| off" estimate from χ(z).
- **A `FOCUS CHECK pinhole` run-sheet row** at the start and end of every spray day.
  - It is captured and analysed for χ, |z| off and the pinhole's diameter.
  - The result is written into that day's runs' metadata (when the focus was checked, χ, and µm/px from the
    pinhole).
- **The same meter focuses the bead and reticle slides,** instead of the eye.

**Why.**
- The new focus rule keeps a slab only ±0.25–0.9 mm deep. If the focal plane sits 1 mm off the spray axis, we
  measure the side of the spray, not its centre.
- Focus by eye was already ~1 mm off on 2026-10-15.
- χ does not tell which side of focus you are on. With the rail scale, step one way and watch χ.

**Where it comes from.**
- The beads: χ(z) = 13 µm + ~8 µm/mm; the 155.8 slide at χ 21 µm; the pours 2.5 mm off focus.
- Blaisot & Yon's edge-width method.

**When the reticle arrives.**
- The PS20 has **dots and squares** in a √2 series from 3.5 µm to 3.5 mm (Ben, 2026-10-15).
- Step a large square and the dots through focus to check the meter's χ → |z| conversion on certified targets.
- Check the pinhole's measured diameter against the reticle's certified scale.

## Phase 2. The smallest size we can confidently measure (current lens)

**What.**
- **A model of our optics:** Blaisot's disc convolved with a Gaussian PSF, using χ0 13 µm and k 8 µm/mm, 10 µm
  pixels with random sub-pixel positions, single-frame noise measured from real frames, and τ from the beads.
  - Simulate d = 10–80 µm inside the focus slab and measure each disc with the 3.0.0 measurement.
  - This gives size bias, scatter, the chance the χ test accepts it, and the chance it is found at all.
- **Check the model against the real beads** at 37 / 56 / 76 µm: it must reproduce their size-against-χ curves,
  static and falling.
- **AI detection by size** from the 20 hand-labelled frames. Use existence only; their sizes are circular, snapped to
  half-max.
- **Per size, the three numbers the literature uses** (Kapulla et al. 2008, a target of opaque discs: 26 discs of
  each of 76 sizes, 0.2–14.7 px):
  - the share found;
  - the scatter (RMS) within each size;
  - the bias against the true size.
  Their lower limit was where detection fell off: ~3 px across for LaVision's DaVis code.
- **d_min** = the smallest size that meets "confident" (decision above). It goes into the profile.
  - Droplets below d_min are counted (for counts and the atomised fraction) but not sized.
  - If needed, the 20–60 µm band's lower edge moves up to d_min.
  - The study also says how far below d_min the χ test itself still works, which is what decides whether a
    small droplet counts as inside the slab.
- **Expected, from published rules of thumb:**
  - ~3 px across to detect (DaVis);
  - ~5 px to resolve (Dantec);
  - an accepted minimum radius of 4 px in a reticle-checked study (Exp. Fluids 2023);
  - PDIA agreed with phase-Doppler above ~25 µm (Kashdan et al. 2007).

  At 10 µm/px this puts d_min near 30–50 µm on the current lens. Our floor today is 22.6 µm (4 px of area,
  ~2.3 px across).
- New script: `AI/Real_Data_Code/size_limit.py`, with a report and a figure.

**Why.**
- Over half the spray reads below our smallest bead, and the bottom 5% sit on the 4 px floor.
- D32, the mean and the band count include sizes nobody can vouch for.

**Where it comes from.**
- The beads (37 µm is the smallest).
- 10 µm pixels and χ0 ≈ 13 µm (about 1.3 px of blur).
- Recall below 50 µm is ~75% (from the benchmark).
- No smaller beads until the 3× lens.

**When the reticle arrives.**
- The PS20's 21 dots and squares run in a √2 series from 3.5 µm to 3.5 mm: 3.5, 4.95, 7.0, 9.9, 14.0, 19.8, 28.0,
  39.6, 56.0, 79.2, 112, 158, 224, 317 µm … 3.5 mm.
- Image the **14, 19.8, 28 and 39.6 µm dots** at focus and through focus.
  - That gives a **measured** small-size limit for opaque discs (our beads look opaque here: no rings), replacing
    the model's extrapolation.
  - The squares of the same sizes show where corners and edges stop being resolved.
  - The 39.6 ↔ 37.36 µm pair ties the dots to the beads.
- `targets.json` gains the full series, each with its shape, from the certificate.
- d_min is updated in profile v2.

## Phase 3. Calibrate the edge and the focus rule (from the beads)

**What.** Using the static through-focus series and the falling pours (`falling_beads.csv`):
- **The edge rule.** Choose whichever gives the smallest bias across sizes for sharp beads:
  - the grey level l* that returns the certified size (static l* 0.27–0.41, the same thing as the +6 µm at
    half-max);
  - or Blaisot's contrast-corrected size at l = 0.61.
- **χ_max: one value for every size,** chosen so sizes hold within the agreed tolerance down to d_min.
  - Blaisot: a single χ_max gives a measurement depth that does not depend on size, so no 1/DOF(d) weighting.
  - Check that this holds; if it doesn't, use per-size depths.
- **The measurement depth for that χ_max,** per size, from the through-focus beads.
- All of it goes into the profile.

- **A defocus look-up table (Ben, 2026-10-15): sizing droplets that are off focus but still measurable.**
  - Inputs per droplet: its apparent size (at the chosen level, or Blaisot's corrected size), blur width χ and
    contrast.
  - Outputs: a corrected size with its uncertainty, and an estimate of |z|.
  - Built from the static through-focus beads (known size and depth), as a correction factor over apparent size × χ,
    interpolated.
  - Checked on the falling beads, which are not used to build it: it must hold ±10% at every size ≥ d_min, up to
    the χ limit it is used to.
  - Two tiers if it passes:
    - χ ≤ χ_sharp: sized directly;
    - χ_sharp < χ ≤ χ_vol: sized through the table;
    - beyond χ_vol: outside the measurement volume.
  - χ_vol is one value for every size (the decision above), so the measurement depth stays the same at every size.
    If the table fails the falling-bead check, 3.0.0 uses the single sharp cut only.
  - **Why:** χ ≤ 15 µm keeps only 7–14% of what is counted now. In spray most droplets sit near χ 18 µm, and the
    table could keep most of them sized, with far better statistics.
  - **Where it comes from:** Blaisot & Yon 2005 (correcting the apparent size of unfocused drops, with a measurement
    volume independent of size); Fdida & Blaisot 2010 (a calibrated PSF, so drops are sized whatever their
    distance from focus); Kashdan et al. 2007 (a blur-width criterion that extends the volume to defocused
    droplets).
  - **Our beads:** Blaisot's corrected size stops the runaway with defocus. 37 µm at 4 mm reads 1.26× against
    half-max's 1.74×; 56 µm at 6 mm reads 1.25× against 1.54×. But it levels off 15–25% high, and that steady
    remainder is what a table measured on our own beads removes.

**Needs** the big-bead through-focus series (rig, ~1 h, focused with the Phase-1 meter) for sizes above 76 µm.
Until then, those sizes are marked provisional, in the table too.

**Why.**
- Today's edge (half-max) reads +3–7 µm large at focus.
- Today's focus test accepts beads 4–7 mm out.
- Ben: one rule for every size.

**Where it comes from.** `report.md`, `optics_calibration_v1.json` and `falling_beads.csv` (2026-10-15).

**When the reticle arrives.**
- Opaque discs from 39.6 to 317 µm, through focus: the edge level and χ_max on certified targets.
- With the scale confirmed, scale and edge are separated for good.
- A second look-up table from the dots. It should match the beads' (the beads look opaque here); any difference
  is the transparency effect. The 14–28 µm dots extend the table below 37 µm.
- That becomes profile v2; re-run the Phase 6 checks.

## Phase 5. Counts per volume, and the atomised fraction

**What.**
- **Concentration:** in-focus droplets per mm³ = count / (field area × measurement depth), with the border correction
  (W·H)/((W−d)(H−d)).
  - The band count becomes a concentration in mm⁻³.
  - Rates use the 3.0.0 ⟨d³⟩.
- **Atomised fraction inside the focus slab:**
  - Filaments and blobs are judged by the same blur-width test, using the edge-width method of `focus_edge.py` along
    their outline.
  - Objects outside the slab leave both the numerator and the denominator.
  - Today's all-objects figure is kept alongside, as decided.
- **Steadiness responses keep counting every detection** (they don't depend on focus), and the figures say so.

**Why.**
- Pure spheres read 62–89% atomised.
- Counts are taken over a 9–15 mm slab that grows with droplet size.

**Where it comes from.**
- The AI-on-spheres result and the through-focus depths.
- `DROPLET_MEASUREMENT_METHOD.md`, problems 2 and 3.

**Validate.**
- The pours must read ≥ 98% atomised.
- On the 20 hand-labelled frames, liquid area against the labels (the classical stage read 1.02× before).

**When the reticle arrives.** Depth measured from certified dots, so the volume in mm³ is traceable.
